What our AI research group found

and what it refused to claim ← Trend Detective

How much of a record rain does the land keep? In 2024 record rain fell on the Sahara and the Sahel. In winter 2025-26 rain ended Morocco's seven-year drought. NASA's GRACE satellites weigh the water held in the ground from orbit. We used them to measure what stayed. We found no published study that had done so.

These are measurements, not a claimed discovery. Our agents and we ran 81 pre-registered tests on data not seen beforehand. Everything that would have made the water result surprising (more water than the rain explains, a new trend) came back not shown, and we say so below. One further test, on air pollution, is reported after the water results.

Re-testing the trends in NASA's own reference paper

The challenge's resource list includes a NASA study of ocean chlorophyll off ten river mouths, measured by the SeaWiFS satellite from 1998 to 2007. It reported significant trends off the Mississippi (decreasing) and the Eel (increasing in summer). We asked: with 24 years of MODIS Aqua data and a test that accounts for the carry-over from one month to the next, are those trends still there?

None of the paper's three significant trends is detected in the longer record. Off Mississippi East, even the 2002-2007 overlap with the paper's period is significant with the paper's method (p = {{ '%.3f'|format(rivers['Mississippi East'].modis_2002_2007.p_uncorrected) }}) and not once carry-over is accounted for (p = {{ '%.3f'|format(rivers['Mississippi East'].modis_2002_2007.p_corrected) }}): the choice of test changes the answer. One river, the Guadalquivir, does show a decrease on MODIS.

"No trend detected" means not significant: small trends are not excluded, and 2002 to 2025 is a different period and sensor from the paper's. Coastal chlorophyll near the Amazon and Ganges is affected by sediment and dissolved matter. These numbers were computed with this app's own code (scripts/river_recheck.py) as an independent check of a longer campaign by our team, which also tested a second satellite (VIIRS) held out from the agents; there, most re-tests were too short to be conclusive and the Guadalquivir decrease was not significant on VIIRS alone (p = 0.19). We found no published re-test of these trends.

Also detected: a human-made change in the air (UAE, 2022)

Traffic and power plants put nitrogen dioxide into the air, and there is less of it on rest days. On 1 January 2022 the United Arab Emirates moved its weekend from Friday-Saturday to Saturday-Sunday. NASA's OMI instrument, which has measured this gas every day since 2004, shows the cleanest day of the week over Dubai and Abu Dhabi moving from Friday to Sunday in that year.

This is a step change in a weekly pattern, not a long-term trend, and it says nothing about tonnes of emissions or health: OMI passes at about 13:30 local time. Registered in docs/preregistrations/3_weekend_shift.md before any data for these cities were downloaded. We found no published measurement of this change. The same test on Oman and Saudi Arabia (2013) also passed, but a control city showed a similar shift without a weekend change, so we do not rely on those two; Algeria (2009) was not shown.

Why you can check this

Agents propose; code decides. Every claim is tested by code on data the agents never see, with the test written down first. A result must repeat on a second, independently processed dataset, survive a correction for the number of tests, and pass a NASA ADS literature search before it can be called new. Nothing passed all of that, so nothing is called new.

Re-run the numbers on this page offline: python -m synaptic reproduce. Full record of every round: docs/research/research_log.md.
